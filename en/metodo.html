Methodological notes

What each series measures, how each indicator is calculated and which reading precautions to keep in mind.

Living document, updated on 17/10/2026. Per-card summaries are in the key notes on the home page.

What each series measures

Migration

People who change their country of residence. The source calls it mig_prev: the flow is identified by the country of previous residence, not of birth. The series is annual and runs from 1990 to 2023.

Visitors

People who cross an international border without changing their country of usual residence. It includes same-day and overnight trips, whatever the reason: tourism, work, visiting relatives, study, health, shopping or transit. This is gtmd2_vflow_int and it runs from 1995 to 2022.

Mobility

The sum of the two previous categories. The source calls it gtmd2_mobflow and defines it literally as international visitors + migrants. Migrants and visitors are disjoint categories: what separates them is whether the crossing changes usual residence, so no person falls into both.

Units and denominators

Crossings are counted, not people

A migrant appears once, in the flow of the year in which they change residence. A visitor, by contrast, may add several arrivals within one year by travelling more than once or visiting several countries. The sum of migrants and visitors is therefore a count of travel events, not of distinct individuals, which is why percentages using it as a denominator need not add up to one hundred.

Trips per migrant

Visitor arrivals divided by migrants in the same corridor and period. It is not a rate per inhabitant: the denominator is those who migrated, not the population. A value of 50 means that for every person who moved there were fifty visitor crossings the other way.

Migrant share

Migrants divided by total mobility, that is migrants plus visitor arrivals. It gives the part of a corridor's crossings made by people who changed residence. It is the exact reciprocal of one plus the ratio above: share = 100 / (1 + ratio). The two readings are the same information on different scales.

Fixed scales across panels

In heatmaps the colour scale is the same across periods. If each panel were rescaled to its own maximum, changes between periods would become invisible. The cost is that a period with low values looks pale.

Grey bands in the series

The years 2022 and 2023 are shaded grey where the migration series may be distorted by the estimation model itself: the model drifts away from the UN DESA stock and then corrects all at once to match the 2024 figure. This appears as one-year spikes. Those years are better read as an average than one by one.

One-year offset

In the migration series, the flow labelled year t is the change between t and t+1: the 2018 Venezuelan peak corresponds to the 2018–2019 movement. Stocks are placed on 1 January.

Coverage and missing data

Visitors

Arrivals come from the tourism statistics each country reports, which are incomplete: only a third to a half of country pairs in the Americas have data in a given year. Sums are a lower bound and corridors without data are not added in as zero. There are no arrivals to Venezuela after 2017.

Migration

The series is a modelled estimate, not an administrative record: it covers all corridors, including those no country reports. That makes it complete but also exposes it to the model artefacts described above.

R4V registrations

R4V counts people registered by the governments of 17 Latin American and Caribbean countries, not people who arrived. It is a lower bound and its jumps may reflect regularisation processes rather than arrivals, as with Colombia in 2022. It does not cover the United States or Spain.

Censuses

Censuses count population by country of birth. Argentina does not ask about nationality, so it does not distinguish people born in Venezuela who migrated with Colombian nationality, for instance. Totals are comparable across censuses, not with flow series.

UN DESA stocks

UN DESA publishes stocks every five years plus an estimate for 2024. Lines joining points in a chart are a visual guide only, not an estimated interpolation.

Reproducibility

How the dashboard is built

Each page is generated from a Python module with a single command: python src/tablero_v3.py web produces the Spanish and English versions in docs/. Figures are Plotly objects written into the HTML itself, with no external dependencies beyond the library. Working data are derived from the original sources and are not versioned in the repository.
