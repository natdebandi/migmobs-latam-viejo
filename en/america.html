Flows between subregions

Annual flows between subregions (period average)

Average annual flow in the period between countries, summed by subregion of origin (left) and destination (right). Migration: people who change their country of residence. Mobility: international visitor trips (up to 2022). Flows between two countries outside the Americas are not included.

Reading. Between 1995 and 2002, on average 0.66 million people per year moved from Central America to Northern America and 0.18 million between South American countries. Hover over each flow to see its value.

Reading. Between 2003 and 2014, on average 0.54 million people per year moved from Central America to Northern America and 0.35 million between South American countries. Hover over each flow to see its value.

Reading. Between 2015 and 2023, on average 0.60 million people per year moved from Central America to Northern America and 1.33 million between South American countries. Hover over each flow to see its value.

Reading. Between 1995 and 2002, there were on average 20.2 million visitor trips per year from Central America to Northern America and 19.6 million between South American countries. Hover over each flow to see its value.

Reading. Between 2003 and 2014, there were on average 27.4 million visitor trips per year from Central America to Northern America and 28.3 million between South American countries. Hover over each flow to see its value.

Reading. Between 2015 and 2022, there were on average 30.9 million visitor trips per year from Central America to Northern America and 37.5 million between South American countries. Hover over each flow to see its value.

Flows involving the Americas, by type

Annual sum of flows with origin or destination in a country of the Americas, in absolute values, by where origin and destination are: both in the same subregion, in different subregions of the Americas, or one outside the Americas. The reading is anchored in 2015, the last complete year before the grey band and the COVID drop.

Reading. In 2015, flows with origin or destination in the Americas totalled 5.4 million people: 1.9 million arrived from outside the continent, 1.6 million moved between American subregions, 1.3 million left for outside, and 0.6 million moved within a single subregion. In 1990 the total was 4.7 million people.

Reading. In 2015, flows with origin or destination in the Americas totalled 351.8 million visitor trips: 47.5 million arrived from outside the continent, 129.7 million moved between American subregions, 53.1 million left for outside, and 121.6 million moved within a single subregion. In 1995 the total was 205.7 million visitor trips. Corridors without data are not added..

Each subregion

Where the flows from each subregion go

Flows originating in the countries of each subregion, by destination: another country of the same subregion, another subregion of the Americas, or outside the Americas. Absolute values; the percentage in the reading is in parentheses and the reference year is 2015.

Reading. In 2015, 1.0 million people left South America: 0.4 million (35%) to another country of the same subregion, 0.3 million (32%) to another subregion of the Americas and 0.3 million (33%) outside the continent. From Central America 0.6 million left and 80% went to another American subregion; from the Caribbean, 0.4 million and 71%. From Northern America, by contrast, 1.4 million and 55% left the continent. Within South America the weight of the subregion itself went from 30% in 1995 to 64% in 2019. Each panel has its own scale.

Reading. In 2015, 63.7 million visitor trips left South America: 46.2 million (73%) to another country of the same subregion, 12.2 million (19%) to another subregion of the Americas and 5.3 million (8%) outside the continent. From Central America 50.0 million left and 76% went to another American subregion; from the Caribbean, 5.4 million and 50%. From Northern America, by contrast, 185.3 million and 25% left the continent. Within South America the weight of the subregion itself went from 77% in 1995 to 72% in 2019. Each panel has its own scale.

Net migration of each subregion

People arriving from countries outside the subregion minus people leaving for countries outside it. Moves between countries of the same subregion cancel out.

Reading. South America went from a net balance of -75,237 people in 2010 to -627,370 in 2018 (red bars: more people leave than arrive).

Source. Gaskin, T. & Abel, G. J. (2026). Deep learning four decades of human migration. Nature. doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10611-7. Annual flow by country of previous residence (mig_prev).

Departures per 1,000 inhabitants of the subregion

Annual flow originating in the countries of the subregion (to any other country) divided by the total population of the subregion, per 1,000. 1995–2022. The South American rate includes Venezuela: the 2018 jump is almost entirely its own, and the reading also gives the series excluding it.

Reading. In 2015, 2.6 people per 1,000 inhabitants were leaving South America; in 2018 the rate reached 8.7 and in 2019 it fell to 7.5. The jump is not the whole subregion: in 2018, 71% of South American departures were from Venezuela. Without Venezuela the rate goes from 2.3 per 1,000 in 2015 to 2.7 in 2018 and 3.6 in 2019, within the range of the other subregions. In 2019 the Caribbean stood at 9.4 per 1,000, Central America at 5.3 and Northern America at 5.0.

Reading. In 2019 South America recorded 162 trips abroad per 1,000 inhabitants, against 156 in 2015; in 2022, with the pandemic, it fell to 91. Northern America is the most mobile, with 598 per 1,000 in 2019.

Mobility and migration

Mobility and migration, by subregion

Two readings of the same pair of numbers, by subregion of origin (rows) and destination (columns), in the chosen period. The ratio divides visitor arrivals by migrants. The share divides migrants by total border crossings, that is migrants plus visitor arrivals: it gives the part of crossings made by people who changed residence.

Reading. Between 1995 and 2002 there were 31 visitor trips for each person who migrated from Central to Northern America. Within South America the ratio was 121 trips per migrant.

Reading. Between 2003 and 2014 there were 51 visitor trips for each person who migrated from Central to Northern America. Within South America the ratio was 85 trips per migrant.

Reading. Between 2015 and 2022 there were 51 visitor trips for each person who migrated from Central to Northern America. Within South America the ratio was 34 trips per migrant, against 121 in 1995–2002: migration within the subregion grew considerably faster than visitor trips.

Reading. Between 1995 and 2002, of every hundred border crossings between Central and Northern America 3.2 were made by people changing residence, one in 32; the rest were visitor arrivals, which involve no change of residence. Within South America the share was 0.8 per cent, one in 122.

Reading. Between 2003 and 2014, of every hundred border crossings between Central and Northern America 1.9 were made by people changing residence, one in 52; the rest were visitor arrivals, which involve no change of residence. Within South America the share was 1.2 per cent, one in 86.

Reading. Between 2015 and 2022, of every hundred border crossings between Central and Northern America 1.9 were made by people changing residence, one in 52; the rest were visitor arrivals, which involve no change of residence. Within South America the share was 2.8 per cent, one in 35.

The grey band in the charts marks 2022–2023, years in which the data may be distorted by the Gaskin and Abel model adjustment.

In the Venezuelan case, the 2018 and 2019 flows cannot be separated precisely. The model estimates the stock at 1 January of each year and the flow is the difference between two consecutive years, so in reality the jump may be spread across both. The size and direction of the change do not depend on that assignment.
