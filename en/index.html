Migration and Mobility in the Americas

Dashboard of migmobs.latam, the Latin American component of the MIGMOBS project — The Orders and Borders of Global Inequality: Migration and Mobilities in Late Capitalism (ERC Advanced Grant, University College Cork; PI: Adrian Favell). It brings together migration and mobility estimates to look at the Americas as a regional system, with a focus on South America and Venezuela.

Subregions of the Americas

  • Northern America. United States, Canada, Bermuda, Greenland and Saint Pierre and Miquelon.
  • Central America. Mexico, Guatemala, Belize, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Costa Rica and Panama.
  • Caribbean. Cuba, the Dominican Republic, Haiti, Jamaica, Puerto Rico, Trinidad and Tobago, the Bahamas, Barbados and 20 other island countries and territories.
  • South America. Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Guyana, Paraguay, Peru, Suriname, Uruguay, Venezuela, French Guiana and the Falkland Islands (Malvinas).

United Nations M49 classification, used by UN DESA and most migration datasets. Mexico is in Central America: the Mexico → United States corridor counts as a flow between subregions. Everything outside the Americas is 'rest of the world'.

Key notes for reading the data

  • Migration and mobility. Migration: people who change their country of residence (Gaskin and Abel). Mobility: international visitor trips (GTMD2); it counts trips, not people.
  • Grey bands (2022–2023). They mark years in which Gaskin and Abel data may be distorted by the estimation model itself: the model drifts away from the UN DESA stock and then corrects all at once to match the 2024 figure. This shows up as one-year spikes, e.g. Mexico → United States or Colombia → Venezuela in 2022. These years are better read as an average than one by one.
  • One-year offset in Gaskin and Abel. The flow labelled with year t is the change between t and t+1: the 2018 Venezuelan peak is the 2018–2019 movement. Stocks are therefore placed on 1 January. The series runs to 2023 (2024 is empty).
  • Missing data in GTMD2. Visitor flows come from the tourism statistics reported by each country, which are incomplete: only a third to a half of country pairs in the Americas have data in a given year. Sums are a lower bound. There are no arrivals to Venezuela after 2017.
  • R4V counts registrations, not people. Only people registered by the governments of 17 Latin American and Caribbean countries: it is a lower bound, and jumps may reflect regularisation processes rather than arrivals (e.g. Colombia in 2022). It does not cover the United States or Spain.
  • UN DESA publishes stocks every five years plus 2024; lines between points are only a guide. Censuses: population by country of birth (Argentina does not ask about nationality).
  • Free movement. Coded by corridor and year from the destination country's perspective. Venezuela is not part of the extended MERCOSUR in this coding. Before/after and with/without comparisons are descriptive, not causal effects.
